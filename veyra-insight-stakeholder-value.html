Stakeholder Value Examples

How one wheelchair-mounted cobot creates different forms of value.

A companion page preserving practical examples across procurement, ownership, daily use, care, maintenance, funding and public interaction.

Reading principle: the same product capability can create a human benefit for the user, an operational benefit for the care organisation and an economic benefit for the payer.
UserCarePayerEcosystem
Primary User and Family

Value in everyday life

01

Act without waiting

Reach products, retrieve dropped objects, carry lightweight items and start activities when the user chooses.

Value: agency and control over the day.
02

Participate and contribute

Help grandchildren bake, hand an item to another person, support meal preparation and join family activities.

Value: belonging and the ability to help others.
03

Reduce avoidable risk

Use support when handling hot items, reaching awkward locations or recovering objects without creating an unstable situation.

Value: safer independence.
04

Preserve dignity

Reduce dependence on others for routine household and personal-care preparation tasks.

Value: privacy, confidence and self-direction.
05

Family peace of mind

Provide practical support between visits and clearer status information when the system detects abnormal operation.

Value: reassurance without constant supervision.
06

Affordable access

Lower-cost actuators can support a lower system price, smaller user contribution or a more achievable lease payment.

Financial example: €1,600 user contribution on an €8,000 installed system at 20% co-payment.
Caregivers and Care Providers

More care, fewer routine chores

01

Professional attention

Shift time from fetching, carrying and basic handling toward assessment, coaching and face-to-face interaction.

Value: skilled time applied where human judgement matters.
02

Capacity release

If 30 minutes per day is released and 65% can be redeployed, the illustrative usable capacity value is €2,503 per year before support overhead.

Financial example: €8,278 five-year NPV after setup and support costs.
03

Informal-care relief

Family members may spend less time on repeated practical assistance and more time in their relationship role.

Value: reduced burden and more meaningful contact.
Insurer, Municipality or Public Payer

Funding value must be measurable

01

Reduced reimbursed support

Routine assistance may be reduced where the user can safely complete tasks with the cobot.

Financial example: €3,194 annual attributed care-cost avoidance using the stated assumptions.
02

Independent living

Greater self-sufficiency may support living at home for longer, but attribution must be established through evidence.

Financial example: €4,000 probability-weighted Year-5 benefit in the illustrative model.
03

Outcome-based reimbursement

Funding can be linked to uptime, actual task use, reduced support hours and user-reported independence rather than hardware ownership alone.

Value: a clearer connection between payment and realised outcomes.
Procurement, OEM, Service and Public Proximity

Value across the supporting ecosystem

01

Robot OEM

Lower actuator cost, mass and integration effort can support a lower bill of materials, a more competitive selling price and product variants.

Value: affordability without relying only on margin reduction.
02

Procurement

A modular platform, transparent lifecycle cost and measurable service levels make offers easier to compare.

Value: better visibility of total cost of ownership.
03

Maintenance Provider

Diagnostics and replaceable modules can shorten fault finding and reduce the need to replace the complete arm.

Value: predictable service and lower downtime.
04

Bystanders

Predictable, force-limited movement and clear status indications help people understand how to interact around the system.

Value: trust and social acceptance.
05

Wheelchair Integrator

Compact, lightweight and power-efficient drives can reduce mounting, energy and packaging compromises.

Value: a more practical integrated product.
06

Society

Broader access can support inclusion, active participation and more efficient use of scarce care capacity.

Value: participation rather than passive support.
